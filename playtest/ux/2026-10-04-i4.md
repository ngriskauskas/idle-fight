# UX Review: i4 Gameplay Results

## Verdict

**Browser status: no UI was browser-seen.** The required Chrome browser tools were not available in this session; all interface findings below are code-only, and run outcomes come from the i4 playtest reports and saved-run data. i4 has a satisfying speed-trigger payoff and a real caster damage-versus-sustain choice in the gameplay evidence, but the interface does not make either mechanism easy to understand at decision time. The highest-value change is to explain that magic mana cost is also its damage multiplier, and preview both the damage gain and resource pressure when choosing caster talents. The 1-second/10-tick speed cooldown is especially uncertain: the playtest infers it, but the reviewed trigger code has no visible cooldown state or explicit shared timer.

## What I looked at

- Read `DESIGN.md`, the newest available evaluation (`2026-10-04-r2.md`), and the i4 caster, fast, and status reports.
- Chose the saved level-8 caster run `i4-caster-805` for the Affinity/Efficiency progression and the level-8 `i4-fast` run for the on-hit speed trigger and pacing. I used their playtest reports and saved-state/status output; I did not load either into a browser.
- Started Vite on the required isolated port `5199`; the page endpoint returned HTTP 200. Browser automation tools were unavailable, so I could not create a private tab, inspect desktop or narrow layouts, take screenshots, or read browser console output. The server was stopped after review.
- Read the relevant current UI and logic in `App.tsx`, `ProgressionBar.tsx`, `CombatantCard.tsx`, `SpellCard.tsx`, `SpellTooltip.tsx`, `TalentPanel.tsx`, `LogPanel.tsx`, `triggerActions.ts`, and the tier-one talent data.

## First five minutes

This section is code-only, not a fresh-player browser session. The main combat view has current enemy/wave/world plus a highest-progress record, character and enemy health bars, mana, spell cards with cast meters, and a pause control. Damage floats include a spell icon and crit label; kill events are also animated, so the action has useful moment-to-moment feedback.

The first likely caster misunderstanding is that `manaCost` sounds like a pure price. In the current magic card formula it also multiplies spell damage. The card shows the resulting damage and a bare mana-cost number, but the meaning of that relationship is not stated where the player chooses a spell or talent. This is the largest decision-language gap in the requested focus.

## Mid-run decisions

The i4 caster report shows a consequential, non-linear choice. With Affinity 3 and Efficiency 2, mana was low enough to block casts 59% of the stretch and average mana was 23%; at Efficiency 3 with the same gear and spell loadout, low-mana time fell to 0% and average mana rose to 84%, while measured kills/minute was nearly unchanged (15.8 to 15.6). Raising Affinity to 4 and equipping the higher-cost Spellbook brought starvation back: 49% low-mana time, 22% average mana, and 14.7 kills/minute. These comparisons are partly confounded by changing progression, but the sustain threshold is clear enough to explain the intended decision.

The interface does not explain that choice. Arcane Affinity lists `manaCost` increases; Mana Efficiency lists `manaRegen` increases. Neither talent has a description that says Affinity raises magic damage by making each spell more expensive, while Efficiency restores casting uptime without directly increasing spell damage. The talent tooltip can display effects, but not the resulting per-spell damage/cost or the expected resource consequence. See [tier1Talents.ts](src/data/talents/tier1Talents.ts#L64) and [TalentPanel.tsx](src/components/TalentPanel.tsx#L139).

Spell costs are only partly legible. Equipped magic cards show a current cost number and calculated damage, but the number has no adjacent "mana per cast" label. The spell tooltip uses base mana cost and base damage values, not the current gear/talent-modified values shown on the card. This makes comparing a new spell's effective cost against the current build awkward, especially when Spellbook and Affinity have pushed the cost far above base. See [SpellCard.tsx](src/components/SpellCard.tsx#L35) and [SpellTooltip.tsx](src/components/SpellTooltip.tsx#L108).

## Combat and death

For the speed build, the `i4-fast` report records 36 Quick Strike hits in the minute before Plate Armor and 85 in the following minute with the armor trigger; the subsequent combined trigger stretch averaged about 1.3 Quick Strike hits/sec. The player cleared from wave 2 through wave 4 in a minute after the trigger came online, then remained stopped by wave-5 deaths to Shadow Assassin and Inferno Lord. This is the intended shape: visibly faster re-clears without deleting the next dangerous wall. The before/after spans different wave starts and are not a controlled damage test.

The spell cards already give each spell a cast-progress bar, and floating combat events show the spell that caused damage. However, `speedBoost` silently advances every spell meter toward ready; there is no proc event, uptime/count, or shared cooldown display in the UI. The player can see faster meters but cannot tell whether a trigger fired, how often it fired, or which limit is currently bounding the loop. `TriggersDisplay` names the trigger and value (for example, "On Hit -> Speed Boost 6") but does not explain its timing or cooldown.

There is also a mechanics/report ambiguity worth resolving before teaching a cooldown: the i4-fast report says the shared 10-fast-tick cooldown appears to bound the loop, while the current reviewed trigger implementation directly advances spell timers and `callTriggers` has no explicit shared cooldown state. I found no cooldown readout in the UI. From the interface alone, a player cannot distinguish a designed one-second lockout from the natural interval imposed by their attack/spell timers. See [triggerActions.ts](src/logic/triggerActions.ts#L62) and [TriggersDisplay.tsx](src/components/TriggersDisplay.tsx#L12).

Damage and crit feedback are useful, but the current log is a sequence of detailed damage entries, kills, and drops rather than a compact speed/proc summary. There is no on-screen kills-per-minute or clear-time comparison in the progress strip. After death, current progress can be compared with the saved highest wave, but the visual feedback for a new best or for the accelerated re-clear is not surfaced as an event in the code reviewed.

## At a glance

Current wave, enemy, world, and highest reached are presented together, which is a strong idle-game glance view. Character and enemy bars expose health and mana; spell bars show time to next cast. The run reports demonstrate meaningful pace changes (caster kill rate rose from 9.3 to 15.8/min across stretches; speed gear enabled a one-minute wave-2-to-4 re-clear), but the interface does not retain a rate or time-per-wave summary that makes growth legible when the player returns.

## Faults

- **Cooldown interpretation is unresolved in current code.** The report attributes a shared 10-tick cooldown to the bounded speed loop, but the reviewed trigger path has no explicit shared cooldown state, and the interface displays none. Verify the intended rule and expose the real limiting interval before presenting a cooldown number to players.
- **Narrow layout risk, not browser-confirmed.** The app shell keeps a fixed 360px sidebar beside the combat area and uses side-by-side content columns without a narrow-screen breakpoint in the reviewed layout. A narrow viewport is likely to leave too little width for combat; this needs an actual device-width browser check.
- No visual, clipping, overlap, tooltip-dismissal, or console fault can be confirmed without a browser session.

The most important structural work is responsive navigation, not a wholesale combat-screen redesign. Keep the desktop split, but below a practical breakpoint replace the permanently visible 360px rail with a compact drawer or bottom tab switcher; put friend and enemy cards in a vertical flow. This lets the player reach spells, talents, and gear without compressing the fight into a narrow strip.

## Quick wins

- Label the spell-card cost number "Mana/cast" and keep it visually distinct from calculated spell damage.
- Add short descriptions to Arcane Affinity and Mana Efficiency that state which side of the damage/sustain trade each serves.
- In magic spell tooltips, show current effective mana cost and damage alongside base values, and explicitly label the cost-to-damage formula.
- Rename or annotate "Mana Cost" in talent/item effect summaries as "Mana cost (also magic damage)" so the stat does not read as downside-only.
- Add a compact "Speed Boost triggered" combat-log entry or count, with no guessed cooldown text until the mechanic is verified.

## Keep

- Current and highest progress are shown together; keep that quick comparison.
- Health, shield, mana, and per-spell cast meters expose useful live state.
- Damage floats identify the spell and crit, and kill feedback makes repeated clears feel active.
- Preserve the real speed-trigger trade: i4-fast's re-clear spike felt rewarding, while the wave-5 bosses remained a wall.
- Preserve the caster build's real tension: Efficiency restored casting uptime, while high Affinity plus Spellbook restored burst potential at a resource cost.

## Not reviewed

- No screens or states were directly seen in a browser. In particular, the fresh first-five-minute experience, talent/spell hover behavior, item comparisons, death presentation, and console were not browser-tested.
- Desktop and narrow viewports were not resized or screenshotted; the narrow-layout observation is based on the app shell code only.
- No new run was started. The review used the existing i4 caster and speed run evidence; the speed trigger was not independently playtested in this session.
