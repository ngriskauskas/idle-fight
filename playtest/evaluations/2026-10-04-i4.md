# Evaluation i4 (2026-10-04)

## Verdict

The i4 results say the speed build's runaway was bounded without losing its fun: Quick Strike plus Plate Armor still produced a satisfying power spike, but the build stopped at wave 5 and died to bosses instead of deleting waves 5-9. However, I cannot credit that result to the reported shared one-second cooldown: the current source contains no cooldown counter or gate, only a one-tick-before-ready clamp. Reconcile the run version with the checked-out source before treating the fix as validated; based on gameplay alone, the speed build is still strong but not dominant. The next balance priority after that reconciliation is making mana pressure and its choices legible, especially for status/hybrid builds.

## Evidence base

- Fresh reports: `2026-10-04-i4-fast.md` (`i4-fast`, seed 804), `2026-10-04-i4-caster.md` (`i4-caster-805`, seed 805; unrelated `i4-caster` was correctly left alone), and `2026-10-04-i4-status.md` (`i4-status`, seed 806), each about 25 minutes.
- Comparators: `2026-10-04-r3-new.md` (`r3-new`, seed 801) and `2026-10-04-r3-caster.md` (`r3-caster`, seed 802), also about 25 minutes. These are useful directional comparisons, not controlled same-seed tests.
- Run summaries confirm reported kills, deaths, killing blows and furthest-wave timestamps. The i4 speed build is the only fresh run to test the Plate Armor trigger for a substantial period. The caster wore it late but has no proc telemetry; status did not test it. No i4 run covered a full hour or progressed beyond wave 5. Visual telegraphs, exact speedBoost activations, and the speed build's legendary axe beyond its final 11 seconds were not tested.
- Source check: `triggerActions.ts` applies each speedBoost to every spell and clamps progress to one tick short of ready; `tickActions.ts` increments spell progress but has no shared cooldown state. A recursive search found no cooldown implementation elsewhere in `src/`. The source therefore contradicts the reports' one-second-cooldown attribution. The source's clamp prevents a spell becoming ready in the same trigger pass; it is not a 10-fast-tick cooldown.

## Problems, ranked

1. **The claimed speedBoost cooldown is absent from this source.** i4-fast's lower observed rate supports a bounded outcome, but the current action still accelerates every spell on each trigger, with no shared one-second gate in either logic file. This makes the main regression test unauditable and leaves a repeatable-hit loop structurally possible. Breaks the intent of earned new waves and raises a philosophy question about a global rule versus tuning the trigger pieces. **Confidence: high on source mismatch; medium on cross-run balance comparison.** Fix kind: reconcile the tested version and tune Plate Armor/Adrenaline Surge values or proc pacing; add trigger telemetry.
2. **Mana pressure still narrows hybrid choices.** i4-status averaged 25% mana and reported being too low to cast 72% of the time; Arcane Missile still supplied 30% of direct damage. In i4-caster, the high-Affinity/Spellbook stretch returned to 49% mana-limited time and 22% average mana, with 14.7 kills/min, while the preceding Mana Efficiency 3 stretch had 0% mana-limited time and 15.6 kills/min. These are short, confounded comparisons, but mana pressure is also a complaint in both r3-caster and i4-fast. The opportunity cost is not consistently legible, and expensive spells can lose their slot identity when starved. Breaks real tradeoffs and build variety. **Confidence: medium.** Fix kind: tune Arcane Affinity, Mana Efficiency, spell costs and/or caster gear pacing; clarify the stat/tooltips.
3. **Early decisions and status combinations arrive late for a 25-minute build.** i4-fast did not receive its defining trigger item until 17:49; its Quick Strike unlock was at 9:38. i4-caster's decisive three-spell package came together around 9:57. i4-status finished level 8 with four unspent spell points, one short of Poison Pool's five-point cost, so the intended status/aura combination was not testable. r3-caster likewise reported points saved without a desired purchase. This leaves long stretches of waiting between distinct build choices, despite progression being present. Breaks “always something to work toward.” **Confidence: medium.** Fix kind: early unlock costs, slot timing and status-piece pacing.
4. **Boss pressure is memorable but concentrated in large hits.** i4 testers repeatedly died to Orc Warlord, Shadow Assassin and Inferno Lord attacks; 68 and 73 damage Inferno Lord hits were the largest i4-fast/caster killing blows. The boss gates felt earned, but these runs found little targeted answer beyond health/defense, and the caster's active healing did not prevent burst deaths. Keep the danger, but ensure available build pieces offer preparation beyond more effective health. Breaks build differentiation if every answer is durability. **Confidence: medium-high across three builds.** Fix kind: relevant defensive/control/status pieces and their pacing, not a global easing of bosses.

## What is working

- The speed trigger remains fun: i4-fast describes many rapid hits feeding Quick Strike and clearing old bosses, while the W5 boss survived and killed the player. Preserve that identity and the Plate Armor's real cost (speed -2/defense tradeoff).
- Difficulty moved back toward earned breakthroughs in the observed i4 runs. Inferno Lord, Orc Warlord and Shadow Assassin created distinct checkpoints; deaths and killing blows were legible in the harness.
- The caster package had three distinct damage/status channels, and Mana Efficiency versus Arcane Affinity was a real short-term sustain-versus-output choice. Poison Stab plus status affixes/Epidemic created substantial poison damage without evidence of a runaway.
- Growth still showed in fast re-clears after death; the reports did not find evidence that player stats automatically rise with wave.

## Against DESIGN.md

- **Pace**: matched. Multi-minute waits and uneven wave gains are acceptable; none of these 25-minute runs proves an insoluble wall.
- **Where power comes from**: matched in the inspected scaling paths: player trigger status uses player stats; enemy scaling is gated to enemies, and the other scale calls affect enemy stats/status/aura or drops. Death returns progress to wave 1 as specified.
- **Fun over balance**: matched by the speed-trigger spike; keep its fun while preventing the old boss-deletion chain.
- **Builds**: partly matched. Fast-hit, caster and poison/status runs had different damage profiles, but status identity leaned on affixes and boss answers remained mostly defensive.
- **Synergy**: partly matched. Quick Strike + Plate Armor and Poison Stab + Epidemic/status gear worked; proc counts and Epidemic's individual contribution remain unobservable.
- **Progression**: partly matched. New slots, talents and drops mattered, but Poison Pool's five-point cost missed the level-8 status run's budget, and trigger gear arrived late.
- **Archetypes**: intended roster is broad, but only some have fresh direct evidence; see below.
- **Checklist for new content**: no new content was assessed. The tested speed trigger does create a different play pattern and meaningful gear tradeoff; the shared cooldown itself, if intended, is a global rule and an exception to the preference to fix progression through build pieces.

## Enemies

Orc Warlord asked for durability and cost repeated deaths in the physical/status runs; Shadow Assassin's Slash made a meaningful gate in the fast/caster runs; Inferno Lord's Inferno was frightening and decisive. Bone Warden Poison Stab was a repeat early endpoint for the caster and status build. These attacks were memorable, but the counters testers described were mostly more health, defense or healing. Goblin King and Bone Warden became routine re-clears, while ordinary wave mixes blurred together. Poison, Curse and burst stood out more than tactical enemy behaviors; no i4 tester reported a mana-disruption enemy or a status-countering enemy. Visual telegraphs were not tested.

## Risks in the mechanics

- **SpeedBoost can still feed itself.** It advances every spell on the combatant, not just the spell that triggered it. Current code only stops at `attackCost.total - 1`; repeated on-hit/on-crit triggers can keep pushing ready time forward. i4 observations do not prove a one-second cooldown exists.
- **Mana costs can scale into a rotation bottleneck.** i4-caster ended with mana-cost stat 43 and its report measured the late spellbook/high-Affinity stretch as mana-limited. It remains possible for added spells to reduce total rotation value rather than add a distinct channel.
- **Player wave scaling**: no player stat scaling was found in the inspected `getScaleMulti` call paths; enemy scaling and loot progression remain separate.

## Open questions

- Which exact source revision produced i4? Was the one-second shared cooldown intended but omitted from this workspace, or did the one-tick readiness clamp alone produce the lower rate? Re-run one comparable fast build with per-combatant cooldown and speedBoost activation telemetry before declaring the runaway fixed.
- Does the bounded fast build still climb past W5 with more time, and does its Quick Strike pace remain fun after 25 minutes? Current evidence ends at W5 and the late legendary weapon had only 11 seconds of combat.
- Does an early mana/status hybrid have a viable route with Poison Pool, and does a mana-efficient Fireball/Ice rotation keep all three spells useful against later bosses?
- Rules issue for the owner: death still resets the player to W1, so each boss death buys another re-clear. This is consistent with DESIGN.md and i4 deaths did not show it making progress impossible, but it is the main source of repeated familiar fights. The shared cooldown, if it is present in the tested build, is also a global timing rule rather than a build-piece adjustment; decide whether that exception is intended.
